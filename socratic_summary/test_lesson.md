# Is It Ever Right to Break a Rule?

<p>A made-up lesson used only to test socratic_summary.</p>

Students read a short story about a student who breaks a school rule to help a friend. The central question is: **Is it ever right to break a rule?**

Three positions are on the board:

- Never break a rule
- Break a rule only to protect someone
- Break any rule that is unfair

## Learning goals

By the end of the seminar, students can:

1. Weigh the intent and the consequences of breaking a rule, and defend a position with evidence from the story.
2. Explain how fairness affects who a rule helps or hurts.
3. Name a specific rule that protects people, and say whom it protects.
4. Discuss as a group: build on what others said, and change a position when the evidence changes.

## Evidence of learning

| Goal | Evidence | What meeting it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Intent and consequences | Entry ticket: a starting side with a reason from the story. Exit ticket: where they started and ended. | A position that names intent or a consequence and cites the story. |
| 2. Fairness | Entry ticket question 2. | Connects fairness to who a rule helps or hurts. |
| 3. Protective rules | Exit ticket extra line: "Name one rule that protects people." | A specific rule and whom it protects. |
| 4. Discussion | Exit ticket Start and End lines. | Says what moved them, or why they held. |

## The handout

- Entry ticket: a starting side and a reason.
- Exit ticket: what they believed walking in, what they believe now, and what moved them.
